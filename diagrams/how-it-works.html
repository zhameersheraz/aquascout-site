How it works

What happens when AQUASCOUT sees fire

Five steps, no jargon. Everything below runs on the phone itself. There is no server doing the thinking, and the camera video never leaves the device.

  1. The phone camera takes a picture

    Point the phone at anything. The app grabs a single frame and shrinks it to 640 × 640 so the next step has a fixed amount of work to do.

  2. The AI looks for flame and smoke

    A small neural network called YOLOv8n reads that picture. It was trained on 17,221 photographs of real fires and smoke, and it answers one question per region of the image: is there fire here, is there smoke here, and how sure am I?

    It runs at about 2 frames per second on a mid-range phone. That is fast enough to watch a room, and it is the same model that scores 0.748 mAP50 on the 4,306-image D-Fire test split.

  3. Boxes are drawn on anything it found

    Anything above the confidence floor gets a labelled rectangle. Above 0.20 confidence the box appears. It is deliberately a low bar, because for a fire alarm a false alarm is an inconvenience and a missed fire is a disaster.

  4. The alert goes out with your location

    The app reads your GPS position and sends one SMS to the emergency contact you saved in Settings. The message says what was seen, how confident the model was, and the coordinates. A 30-second cooldown stops it texting repeatedly.

    You can also press and hold REPORT FIRE for 1.5 seconds to send the same alert by hand, without waiting for the AI.

  5. The rover can carry water to the fire awaiting parts

    A separate Bluetooth rover can be driven from the app and carries two cameras and a water pump. It is optional: the alert in steps 1 to 4 works with no rover connected at all.

    The pump and the cameras are in the build plan but the components have not arrived, and the rover's motor driver failed, so no movement is claimed here.

Reading the screen

  1. Solid orange box — the model is confident this is flame.
  2. Dashed grey box — smoke. Softer and wider than flame, so it is easier to confuse with fog or steam.
  3. The percentage — how sure the model is. It is a confidence score, not a physical measurement.
  4. Frames per second — how fast it is re-checking the scene. Lower is more laggy.

What actually leaves the phone

A label, a confidence number and a set of coordinates. Never the video. Camera frames are analysed on the device and discarded. If you are signed in, an alert record is written to Firebase so your history follows you to a new phone. That is the entire list.
